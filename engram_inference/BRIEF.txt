STACK was asked to rewrite a set of query cells while attending to a separate set of context cells, using the published Stack-Large-Aligned checkpoint and the tutorial generation settings of five iterative steps, a prompt fraction of 0.25 and a context fraction of 0.4. The checkpoint expects raw counts and applies its own log transform. Its window holds 512 cells, so each split kept 333 context cells and 179 query cells drawn from the real matrices. Every output file is a new count matrix on those same 179 query cells, together with the per-cell score from the last generation step. The counts are draws from the model's negative binomial, which means the population pattern is the thing worth reading and a single cell's exact numbers will move if the seed changes.

For the cortical organoids the query was 179 intermediate progenitor cells from the five-month sample of the Uzquiano and Arlotta atlas, and the context was apical radial glia, once from day 23 and once from five months. The generated population still looks like a cortical progenitor profile. Average log-transformed expression agrees with the measured intermediate progenitors at a Pearson correlation of 0.87 under the young radial glia and 0.86 under the five-month radial glia, and the two generations agree with each other at 0.94. The genes that lead both the measurement and the generation are the genes that lead this atlas, including MALAT1, EEF1A1 and TMSB4X, and 65 of the 100 strongest genes are shared. Library size stays close to the measurement, with medians of roughly 2,600 and 2,700 counts against 2,842 in the query. Matched cell by cell, the median correlation falls to 0.47 and 0.40, which is what a fresh count draw looks like once the average program is in the right place. The principal component plot is where the prompt still shows: measured intermediate progenitors sit between the two radial-glia ages, cells generated from day-23 radial glia fall in with the day-23 glia, and cells generated from five-month radial glia fall in with the five-month glia. The same pull appears on individual genes. EOMES is present in the measured progenitors, with a mean count of 1.9, and is absent in the radial glia; the young context keeps it at 1.7 and the older context lets it fall to 0.3. VIM is lifted in both generations to a mean near 23, compared with 3.4 in the measured progenitors. TUBB3 is essentially absent in these cells and is introduced by the day-23 context, at a mean of 6.8.

For sci-Plex the query was 179 untreated MCF7 nuclei at 24 hours, and the context was A549 nuclei from the same experiment, either vehicle or one of Ki8751, tranylcypromine and GSK J1 at 10 nM. Those three compounds are the ones with the most cells in both lines at the dose that itself contained the most compounds clearing 200 cells per line, so they were chosen for sample size. The matrix is single-nucleus sci-RNA-seq, and in these MCF7 cells MALAT1, GAPDH and ACTB are essentially undetected, while the deepest genes are SACM1L, GRIN3A and LRIG2. The generations do not recover that profile. Correlation of average expression with the measured query is 0.02 under every context, the rank correlation is 0.12, and the top 100 genes do not overlap. The four generations do agree with one another, at a correlation of 0.99, and the drug-versus-vehicle difference is small beside the profile. That difference lines up with neither the drug effect measured in MCF7 nor the drug effect measured in A549, where the correlations stay between about -0.01 and 0.03. In this sample of 200 cells per group the two measured lines also do not share a drug shift with each other, so there was little common signature for a prompt to carry from A549 onto MCF7.

Read as a pair, the checkpoint can remain inside a cortical progenitor program and let the age of the radial glia tug the generated cells toward the context it was shown, and on this window it does not turn an A549 drug context into a recognizable MCF7 profile. Both statements come from one model window of real cells, rather than from the full organoid atlas or the full screen.
